ANEP-01

Azzopardi Ankh Energy Pyramid

A hand-wound copper instrument, built by folding the ankh's three sides inward into a pyramid over a palm handle at the loop.

status: experimental — hypothesis, not proven science

The build

ANEP-01 started as a direct response to a sensation: a felt pull and push between open palms, tested and re-tested by hand before any device existed. The wire came after the feeling, not before it — built as a physical instrument to hold and test that sensation rather than just describe it.

The form borrows the ankh's oldest structure — a loop above a stem — but instead of leaving the arms flat, the three sides fold inward and converge at a single point above the hand. The loop becomes the grip; the folded sides become a pyramid rising from the palm.

Where the shape has been before

Nothing in the historical record combines all of this exactly — but each piece has a lineage of its own.

Ancient Egypt

The ankh

A loop-and-stem symbol of life, held by its loop and extended hand to hand. Its origin is debated — sandal strap, knot, fertility sign — but the loop was always the part that got held. Copper carried the same association with life and permanence.

1779

Animal magnetism

Mesmer's "passes" — slow, deliberate hand movements meant to direct a felt fluid toward another person. The first systematic hand-based technique built around a sensed force.

1845

Odic force

Reichenbach's proposed force, said to emanate from the hands and be perceptible to certain "sensitives" under the right conditions.

Mid-20th c.

Pavlita's generators

Hand-built sculptural forms in metal, wood, and paper, said to store and release a person's own bioenergy. The closest known precedent for a hand-shaped wire instrument.

1973

Pyramid power

Flanagan's claim that pyramid geometry itself concentrates ambient energy — the first time the shape, rather than the material, carried the theory.

1991

Light-Life rings

Spurling's twisted copper loops, wound by hand and built specifically to be held against the body's field.

2026

ANEP-01

Ankh geometry, folded to a pyramid, wound in copper — built to test a sensation directly rather than to make a claim about it.

Where this comes from

ANEP-01 sits inside a larger line of inquiry — Anima Sol, the Theory of Everything work that treats the Sun as Giver, Earth as Vessel, and living beings as the emergent third term between them. Part of that work has involved direct, personal testing: holding the palms apart and noting what's felt — a pull when they face each other, a push when turned the other way.

ANEP-01 is the first physical instrument built for that testing, rather than just bare hands. It doesn't confirm the sensation has an external cause — it gives the exercise a fixed, repeatable form to test against.

labeled explicitly as hypothesis, not proven science — consistent with how the rest of the Anima Sol / Zion Codex work is presented.
